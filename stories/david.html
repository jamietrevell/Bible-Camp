COURAGE AND FAITH

David: The Shepherd Who Became King

1 Samuel 16–31; 2 Samuel 1–7

David began as a young shepherd looking after his father's sheep. He was not the strongest or most obvious choice for greatness, but God saw something special in his heart.

Samuel Anoints a New King

Israel's first king was Saul. But Saul had repeatedly disobeyed God, and God told the prophet Samuel that He had chosen someone else to become king.

God sent Samuel to Bethlehem, to the household of Jesse. Jesse presented his sons, beginning with the eldest. Samuel initially thought one of the older sons might be God's chosen king.

But God reminded Samuel that people look at outward appearances, while the Lord looks at the heart.

Jesse's youngest son, David, was still out tending the sheep. When he was brought before Samuel, God identified him as the chosen one.

Samuel anointed David with oil, and the Spirit of the Lord came powerfully upon him.

A Giant Named Goliath

Some time later, the Israelites were at war with the Philistines. A Philistine warrior named Goliath challenged the Israelite army to send someone to fight him.

Goliath was enormous, heavily armed, and experienced in battle. For forty days, he challenged Israel, and nobody was willing to face him.

David arrived at the Israelite camp to bring food to his older brothers. When he heard Goliath insulting the Israelite army and defying God, he was astonished that nobody would confront him.

David volunteered to fight. King Saul initially objected, pointing out that David was only a young man while Goliath had been a warrior for years.

David explained that he had protected his father's sheep from lions and bears. He trusted that God would help him face Goliath too.

Five Smooth Stones

Saul offered David his armour, but David was uncomfortable wearing it. Instead, he took his shepherd's staff, his sling, and five smooth stones from a stream.

Goliath laughed when he saw the young shepherd approaching him. David answered that he came in the name of the Lord.

David placed a stone in his sling and launched it towards Goliath. The stone struck the giant in the forehead, and Goliath fell face down to the ground.

David had defeated the seemingly unbeatable warrior. The Philistine army fled, and the Israelites celebrated their victory.

David and Jonathan

After the victory, David became well known throughout Israel. He also formed a close friendship with Jonathan, King Saul's son.

Jonathan loved David as a close friend. The two made a covenant of friendship, and Jonathan gave David his robe, armour, sword, bow, and belt.

Their friendship remained strong even when circumstances became dangerous.

Saul Becomes Jealous

As David's popularity grew, Saul became increasingly jealous. After hearing people praise David's victories, Saul began to fear that David would take his throne.

Saul tried to kill David several times. David was forced to flee and spend years moving from place to place to escape the king's anger.

Jonathan secretly helped David and warned him when Saul intended to harm him. Despite the danger, David refused to take revenge against Saul when he had opportunities to do so.

David Becomes King

Eventually, Saul and Jonathan died in battle against the Philistines. David mourned deeply for both of them.

David was first anointed king over Judah. Later, the tribes of Israel recognised him as king over all Israel.

David captured Jerusalem and made it his capital. He became a powerful king and led Israel through many battles and important changes.

A Promise to David

David wanted to build a permanent house for the Ark of the Covenant. Instead, God sent the prophet Nathan to give David a promise.

God promised that David's royal house and kingdom would endure. His descendants would continue his line, and the promise became an important part of Israel's history.

David was not a perfect man. He made serious mistakes later in his life, but he also became known for his prayers, songs, and deep devotion to God.

What Can We Learn?

  • Courage is not about physical size or strength.
  • God values character more than outward appearance.
  • True friendship involves loyalty and sacrifice.
  • We should resist jealousy and the desire for revenge.
  • Even important leaders make mistakes and need to take responsibility for them.

Think About It!

David faced a challenge that everyone else thought was impossible. What is something that seems frightening to you, but that you might be able to face with courage?
