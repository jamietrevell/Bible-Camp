The Creation Story

How God created the heavens, the earth and everything in them.

📖 Genesis 1–2

Reading time: Approximately 5 minutes

Before the Beginning

Have you ever looked up at the night sky and wondered how the stars got there? Or watched the waves roll towards a beach and wondered where the oceans came from? What about the trees, the mountains, the animals and the people around you?

The Bible begins with an account of how everything came to be. In the book of Genesis, we are introduced to God as the Creator of the universe. Before the world existed as we know it, God was already there.

This is the story of creation.

Day One: Let There Be Light

In the beginning, the earth was formless and empty. Darkness covered the deep waters, and the Spirit of God was hovering over them.

Then God spoke.

"Let there be light."

And there was light.

God saw that the light was good. He separated the light from the darkness. He called the light Day and the darkness Night.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the first day.

Day Two: The Sky Above

On the second day, God made an expanse to separate the waters. He called this expanse Sky.

Above the earth, the sky stretched out, creating a space between the waters below and the waters above.

The world was beginning to take shape. What had once been formless was becoming an ordered place.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the second day.

Day Three: Land, Seas and Plants

God gathered the waters together so that dry ground could appear. He called the dry ground Land, and the gathered waters Seas.

Then God spoke again, commanding the earth to produce vegetation.

Soon, the land was filled with plants, grasses, trees and all kinds of vegetation. Trees grew and produced fruit, while plants produced seeds of their own kinds.

The earth was no longer an empty place. It was becoming a world filled with life and colour.

God saw that it was good.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the third day.

Day Four: The Sun, Moon and Stars

On the fourth day, God made the lights in the sky.

He created the greater light to govern the day and the lesser light to govern the night. He also made the stars.

The sun would provide light during the day, while the moon and stars would shine at night. These lights would help mark the seasons, days and years.

The heavens were filled with countless stars, each part of the wonderful world God was creating.

God saw that it was good.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the fourth day.

Day Five: Life in the Water and Sky

The waters were no longer empty.

God created the great creatures of the sea and all kinds of living things that moved through the waters. Fish of different shapes and sizes filled the oceans and rivers.

Then God created birds to fly across the sky. They filled the air with movement and sound.

God blessed them and told them to multiply and fill the seas and the skies.

God saw that it was good.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the fifth day.

Day Six: Animals and Humanity

On the sixth day, God filled the land with living creatures.

There were livestock, wild animals and creatures that moved along the ground. The earth was now filled with an extraordinary variety of life.

But God was not finished.

God said, "Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness."

And so, God created human beings in His image. He created them male and female, blessed them and gave them responsibility over the other living creatures of the earth.

God gave them plants and fruit for food and entrusted them with caring for the world He had made.

When God looked at everything He had created, He saw that it was very good.

Evening came, and morning followed. This was the sixth day.

Day Seven: A Day of Rest

By the seventh day, the heavens and the earth and everything in them had been completed.

God rested from His work of creation. He blessed the seventh day and made it holy because on that day He rested from all the work He had done.

The world was complete.

The sky stretched above the earth, the seas were filled with life, plants covered the land, birds flew overhead and animals roamed the earth. Humanity had been created in God's image.

Everything had its place in the world God had made.

The Garden of Eden

Genesis 2 gives us a closer account of the creation of humanity and introduces us to a special place: the Garden of Eden.

God formed the first man, Adam, from the dust of the ground and breathed the breath of life into his nostrils. Adam became a living being.

God planted a garden in Eden and placed Adam there. The garden was filled with beautiful trees, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

God gave Adam the responsibility of working in the garden and taking care of it. He also gave him a command concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

God then said that it was not good for the man to be alone. He created the animals and brought them to Adam, who gave them names.

But among all the animals, no suitable companion was found for him.

God caused Adam to fall into a deep sleep. From his side, God formed a woman and brought her to him.

Adam recognised her as one who was like him, and the two became the first human couple.

They were together in the garden, living in the world God had created.

And So, the World Began...

The creation account is one of the most important stories in the Bible. It introduces us to God as the Creator of all things and establishes the special place of humanity within creation.

From the smallest plant to the vastness of the night sky, the world is presented as something God made with purpose.

And at the end of the account, we are reminded of something wonderful: God looked at everything He had made, and it was very good.

Meet the Characters

  • God: The Creator of the heavens and the earth.
  • Adam: The first man, formed from the dust of the ground.
  • Eve: The first woman, created as a companion for Adam.

What Can We Learn?

  1. Creation has value. The world is presented as God's creation, something worth appreciating and caring for.
  2. Human beings have dignity. Humanity is described as being made in God's image.
  3. We have responsibility. Adam was entrusted with caring for the garden. We can learn from this the importance of looking after the world around us.
  4. Rest matters. The seventh day reminds us that rest is an important part of life.
  5. There is beauty in creation. The story encourages us to appreciate the world and the life within it.

Campfire Reflection 🔥

Take a moment to think about what you have read.

If you could have witnessed one of the six days of creation, which day would you choose, and why?

What is one thing in the natural world that makes you appreciate creation?
