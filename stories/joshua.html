COURAGE AND LEADERSHIP

Joshua and the Battle of Jericho

Numbers 13–14; Joshua 1–6; 24

After Moses' death, a new leader was chosen to guide the Israelites into the Promised Land. His name was Joshua, and he would face challenges that required courage, faith, and obedience.

A New Leader

Moses had led the Israelites out of Egypt and through the wilderness. But after many years, he died before the people entered the Promised Land.

God appointed Joshua, the son of Nun, to lead the Israelites. Joshua had served Moses faithfully and had already shown his courage and trust in God.

God told Joshua to be strong and courageous. He promised to be with him just as He had been with Moses.

The Twelve Spies

Years earlier, while the Israelites were still travelling through the wilderness, Moses had sent twelve men to explore Canaan.

The spies returned carrying evidence of the land's abundance, including a large cluster of grapes. They reported that the land was fertile, but they also described its fortified cities and powerful inhabitants.

Ten of the spies were frightened and discouraged the people from entering. But Joshua and Caleb believed that God could help them take the land.

The Israelites listened to the fearful report and refused to trust God. As a result, that generation wandered in the wilderness for forty years. Joshua and Caleb were the exceptions among the adults who had left Egypt.

Rahab Helps the Spies

Before crossing into Canaan, Joshua sent two spies to explore the city of Jericho. They entered the house of a woman named Rahab.

When the king of Jericho heard that Israelite spies were in the city, he sent men to search for them. Rahab hid the spies on her roof beneath stalks of flax and told the king's men that the visitors had already left.

Rahab explained to the spies that the people of Jericho had heard about the wonders God had done for Israel. She believed that the Lord was God in heaven above and on earth below.

She asked the spies to spare her family when the Israelites attacked the city. They agreed, telling her to tie a scarlet cord in her window as a sign.

Crossing the Jordan River

The Israelites prepared to cross the Jordan River. At that time, the river was overflowing its banks.

The priests carrying the Ark of the Covenant stepped into the water. The river stopped flowing upstream, and the people crossed on dry ground.

After everyone had crossed, Joshua instructed twelve men, one from each tribe, to take stones from the riverbed. These stones were arranged as a memorial so that future generations would remember what God had done.

The Walls of Jericho

Jericho was a strongly fortified city. Its walls seemed impossible to overcome, but God gave Joshua unusual instructions.

The Israelites were to march around the city once each day for six days. Seven priests would walk before the Ark of the Covenant, carrying trumpets. Armed men would march ahead of them, while others followed behind.

The people were instructed to remain silent. They were not to shout or make a sound until the appointed time.

On the seventh day, they marched around Jericho seven times. When the priests sounded the trumpets, Joshua told the people to shout.

The people shouted, the trumpets sounded, and the walls of Jericho collapsed.

The Israelites entered the city. Rahab and her family were spared, just as the spies had promised.

Joshua's Final Challenge

Joshua continued to lead the Israelites as they settled in the land. Eventually, when he was old, he gathered the people together and reminded them of everything God had done for them.

He challenged them to remain faithful and choose whom they would serve. Joshua declared that, whatever others chose, he and his household would serve the Lord.

Joshua's leadership had helped guide Israel through a major turning point in its history. His story became a reminder of courage, commitment, and faith.

What Can We Learn?

  • Courage means trusting God even when a challenge looks impossible.
  • Fear can prevent people from taking important steps.
  • Rahab's story reminds us that faith can come from unexpected people.
  • Remembering past help can strengthen us for future challenges.
  • Our choices reveal what we value and whom we serve.

Think About It!

Joshua and the Israelites followed instructions that probably seemed unusual. Can you think of a time when patience and following a plan helped you accomplish something difficult?
