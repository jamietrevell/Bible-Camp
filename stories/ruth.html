LOYALTY AND KINDNESS

Ruth: A Story of Loyalty and Love

Ruth 1–4

When tragedy left two women without their husbands, one made a remarkable choice. Ruth left behind her homeland to remain beside her mother-in-law, Naomi. Her loyalty would lead to a new beginning.

A Family in Moab

During the time of the judges, a famine struck the land of Judah. A man named Elimelech travelled from Bethlehem to Moab with his wife, Naomi, and their two sons, Mahlon and Chilion.

While they were living in Moab, Elimelech died. Naomi's sons later married two Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth.

Sadly, both sons also died. Naomi was left without her husband or sons, with only her two daughters-in-law beside her.

Ruth's Remarkable Promise

Naomi heard that the famine in Judah had ended and decided to return to Bethlehem. She encouraged Orpah and Ruth to remain in Moab and return to their own families.

Orpah eventually said goodbye, but Ruth refused to leave Naomi.

Ruth made a heartfelt promise:

"Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God."
— Ruth 1:16

Ruth chose to leave her homeland and travel with Naomi to Bethlehem. She was determined to remain with her even though the future was uncertain.

A New Life in Bethlehem

When Naomi and Ruth arrived in Bethlehem, it was the beginning of the barley harvest.

They needed food, so Ruth went to gather leftover grain in the fields. This was a provision for people who were poor or without family support.

As it happened, Ruth entered a field belonging to a wealthy man named Boaz, who was related to Naomi's late husband.

Boaz Shows Kindness

Boaz noticed Ruth working in his fields and asked his workers about her. They told him about her loyalty to Naomi and how she had left her homeland to care for her.

Boaz treated Ruth kindly. He told her to remain in his fields, instructed his workers not to harm her, and made sure she had water to drink.

He even instructed his workers to leave extra grain for her to collect.

Ruth was surprised by his kindness and asked why he was treating her so generously. Boaz explained that he had heard about everything she had done for Naomi.

Ruth returned home with plenty of grain and told Naomi about Boaz. Naomi recognised that he was a relative who could help protect their family's future.

A Request for Help

Naomi wanted to find security for Ruth. She advised her to approach Boaz and ask him to act as their family's guardian-redeemer, a relative who could help preserve the family's land and future.

Ruth followed Naomi's instructions. Boaz was moved by her request and praised her character. He agreed to help, but explained that another relative had a closer legal right to act first.

A Promise Kept

Boaz went to the town gate, where important community matters were settled. He spoke with the closer relative and explained the situation.

The relative declined the responsibility, allowing Boaz to become the family redeemer. The agreement was witnessed and publicly confirmed.

Boaz married Ruth, and they had a son named Obed. Naomi's life, which had once been filled with grief, was now filled with joy.

A Remarkable Family Legacy

Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of King David.

Ruth, a woman from Moab, became part of the family line through which King David and, according to the New Testament, Jesus Christ would come.

Her story began with loss and uncertainty, but it became a story of loyalty, kindness, and a new beginning.

What Can We Learn?

  • Loyalty means standing beside people when life becomes difficult.
  • Small acts of kindness can make a tremendous difference.
  • People from different backgrounds deserve dignity and respect.
  • Hard beginnings do not determine how the rest of our lives will unfold.
  • Ruth's story reminds us that faithfulness and compassion matter.

Think About It!

Ruth stayed with Naomi when she could have chosen an easier path. What does loyalty mean to you, and how can you show it to the people you love?
