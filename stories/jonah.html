OBEDIENCE AND MERCY

Jonah and the Great Fish

Jonah 1–4

God gave a prophet named Jonah an important mission. But Jonah did not want to carry it out. Instead, he boarded a ship and sailed in the opposite direction, beginning an adventure he would never forget.

A Message from God

Jonah was a prophet who lived in Israel. One day, God told him to travel to Nineveh, a great city, and warn its people that their wickedness had come to God's attention.

Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire. Its people were known for their cruelty, and Jonah did not want to deliver God's message to them.

Instead of travelling to Nineveh, Jonah went down to the port of Joppa. He found a ship sailing to Tarshish, paid his fare, and boarded it.

He was trying to escape from the task God had given him.

A Terrible Storm

Once the ship was far out at sea, God sent a powerful storm. The wind became so fierce that the sailors feared the ship would break apart.

They threw cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. Each sailor cried out to his own god for help, but the storm continued.

Meanwhile, Jonah had gone below deck and fallen asleep. The captain found him and woke him, telling him to call upon his God.

The sailors decided to cast lots to discover who was responsible for the disaster. The lot fell on Jonah.

They questioned him, asking who he was, where he came from, and why the storm had fallen upon them.

Jonah explained that he was a Hebrew and worshipped the Lord, the God who made the sea and the land. He admitted that he was running away from God's instructions.

The sailors became terrified. They asked Jonah what they should do to calm the sea. Jonah told them to throw him overboard.

At first, the sailors tried desperately to row back to land. But the storm grew worse. Finally, they prayed to the Lord, asking not to be held responsible for Jonah's life, and threw him into the sea.

Immediately, the storm stopped. The sailors were amazed and worshipped God.

Swallowed by a Great Fish

Jonah sank beneath the waves. But God had prepared a great fish to swallow him. Jonah remained inside the fish for three days and three nights.

In the darkness, Jonah prayed. He described how he had been overwhelmed by the sea and how he felt as though he had reached the depths of the earth.

He remembered God and thanked Him for hearing his prayer. Jonah recognised that salvation belonged to the Lord.

After three days, God commanded the fish to release Jonah. The fish brought him onto dry land.

Jonah Goes to Nineveh

God spoke to Jonah a second time, telling him to go to Nineveh and deliver the message.

This time, Jonah obeyed. He travelled to the great city and announced that in forty days Nineveh would be overturned.

The people of Nineveh believed the warning. News reached the king, who rose from his throne, removed his royal robes, and covered himself with sackcloth. He called for everyone, including the animals, to fast.

The king urged the people to turn away from their evil behaviour and violence. He hoped that God might show them mercy.

God saw that the people had turned away from their wicked ways. He had compassion on them and did not bring the destruction He had warned them about.

Jonah's Anger

Instead of being happy that the people had changed, Jonah became angry. He had not wanted Nineveh to be forgiven.

Jonah admitted that he had run away because he knew God was gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, and full of love.

He went outside the city and sat down, hoping to see what would happen.

God caused a leafy plant to grow over Jonah and provide shade from the hot sun. Jonah was very happy about the plant.

But the next day, God sent a worm that damaged the plant, and it withered. When the sun became hot and the wind blew, Jonah grew uncomfortable and angry again.

A Lesson About Compassion

God asked Jonah whether he had any right to be angry about the plant. Jonah insisted that he did.

God then explained that Jonah had cared deeply about a plant he had not grown or created. Yet he had been unwilling to show compassion towards the many people living in Nineveh.

God reminded Jonah that the people of Nineveh mattered too. He cared about them, even though they had done wrong.

The story ends with God's question, leaving readers to think about mercy, forgiveness, and compassion.

What Can We Learn?

  • Running away from a responsibility does not make it disappear.
  • We can pray to God even when we have made mistakes.
  • It is never too late to choose to do what is right.
  • Everyone deserves the opportunity to change.
  • We should not let anger or prejudice prevent us from showing compassion.
  • Mercy means caring about people, even when they have done wrong.

Think About It!

Jonah wanted God to forgive him when he was in trouble, but he struggled to accept that God could forgive the people of Nineveh too. Why is it sometimes easier to want forgiveness for ourselves than to offer it to others?
