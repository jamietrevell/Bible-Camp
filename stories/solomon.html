WISDOM AND CHOICES

Solomon: The Wisest King

1 Kings 1–11; 2 Chronicles 1–9

When Solomon became king of Israel, God offered him an extraordinary opportunity: he could ask for whatever he wanted. His answer would make him famous for generations.

A Young King

Solomon was the son of King David and Bathsheba. When David grew old, Solomon became king of Israel. Although he had inherited a powerful kingdom, he was still young and had much to learn.

Solomon loved God and followed the instructions his father had given him. One day, he went to Gibeon to offer sacrifices.

That night, God appeared to Solomon in a dream and told him to ask for whatever he wanted.

A Request for Wisdom

Solomon could have asked for wealth, a long life, or victory over his enemies. Instead, he asked God for an understanding heart so that he could govern the people wisely and distinguish right from wrong.

God was pleased with his request. Because Solomon had asked for wisdom rather than personal riches or power, God promised to give him wisdom beyond that of anyone else. God also promised him riches and honour.

Solomon woke from the dream and returned to Jerusalem. His reign would soon become known for extraordinary wisdom.

The Two Mothers

One day, two women came before King Solomon. Both lived in the same house and had recently given birth to sons.

During the night, one of the babies died. The two women disagreed about which child was still alive. Each claimed that the living baby was hers.

There were no witnesses to settle the dispute, so Solomon had to find a way to discover the truth.

He ordered that a sword be brought and proposed dividing the living child in two, giving half to each woman.

One woman accepted the proposal, but the other immediately begged the king to spare the baby, even if that meant giving him to the other woman.

Solomon recognised that the woman who would rather surrender her child than see him harmed was the real mother. He ordered that the baby be given to her.

When the people of Israel heard about the judgment, they were amazed by the wisdom God had given their king.

Building the Temple

David had wanted to build a permanent house for God, but God had told him that his son would undertake that work.

Solomon began an enormous building project in Jerusalem: the construction of the Temple.

Skilled workers gathered materials, including cedar wood, stone, gold, and bronze. The building was carefully designed and decorated.

After years of work, the Temple was completed. Solomon gathered the people of Israel for its dedication ceremony.

The Ark of the Covenant was brought into the Most Holy Place. A cloud filled the Temple, and Solomon offered a prayer asking God to hear His people whenever they prayed.

The Queen of Sheba

News of Solomon's wisdom and wealth spread far beyond Israel. The Queen of Sheba heard about him and travelled a great distance to test him with difficult questions.

She brought gifts of gold, spices, and precious stones. Solomon answered all her questions, and she was astonished by his wisdom and the splendour of his kingdom.

She praised God for placing Solomon on the throne and recognised the remarkable prosperity of his kingdom.

When Wisdom Was Not Enough

Although Solomon began his reign with great devotion, his later choices brought serious consequences.

He married many foreign women, and some led him towards the worship of other gods. Solomon's heart was no longer fully devoted to the Lord.

God was displeased with this disobedience and declared that much of the kingdom would be taken from Solomon's descendants. However, part of the kingdom would remain with his family because of God's promise to David.

After Solomon's death, the kingdom eventually divided into two: the northern kingdom of Israel and the southern kingdom of Judah.

Solomon's life reminds us that wisdom is valuable, but it must be accompanied by humility and faithful choices.

What Can We Learn?

  • Wisdom is more valuable than simply having wealth or power.
  • Good leaders should seek justice and understanding.
  • A loving heart is often revealed through difficult choices.
  • Being wise does not automatically mean making wise decisions every time.
  • Our choices can have consequences for ourselves and others.
