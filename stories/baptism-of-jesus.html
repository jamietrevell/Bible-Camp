FAITH AND NEW BEGINNINGS

The Baptism of Jesus

Matthew 3:1–17; Mark 1:1–11; Luke 3:1–22; John 1:19–34

Before Jesus began His public ministry, He travelled to the Jordan River to meet a man who had been preparing the people for His arrival: John the Baptist.

A Voice in the Wilderness

In the wilderness of Judea, a prophet named John began preaching to the people. He wore clothing made from camel's hair, tied with a leather belt, and ate locusts and wild honey.

John had a special message. He told the people to repent, turn away from their sins, and prepare for the coming of God's kingdom.

People travelled from Jerusalem, Judea, and the surrounding region to hear him. Many confessed their sins and were baptised by John in the Jordan River.

John made it clear that he was not the promised Messiah. He was preparing the way for someone greater than himself.

John's Message About the Coming One

John told the people that someone more powerful than he was coming. John was not even worthy to carry that person's sandals.

He explained that while he baptised with water, the one who was coming would baptise with the Holy Spirit and fire.

John knew that his mission was to point people towards Jesus, not to draw attention to himself.

Jesus Arrives at the Jordan

One day, Jesus travelled from Galilee to the Jordan River, where John was baptising people. Jesus came to John and asked to be baptised.

John was surprised. He knew that Jesus was greater than he was and felt that he should be the one receiving baptism from Jesus.

He asked Jesus why He had come to him.

Jesus explained that it was right for them to fulfil all righteousness. John understood and agreed to baptise Him.

The Heavens Open

Jesus entered the water, and John baptised Him. As Jesus came up out of the water, something extraordinary happened.

The heavens opened, and the Spirit of God descended like a dove and rested upon Him.

Then a voice came from heaven, saying: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."

It was a remarkable moment. Jesus' identity as God's Son was publicly affirmed, and the Holy Spirit was revealed in a visible sign.

The Beginning of His Ministry

After His baptism, Jesus was led by the Spirit into the wilderness, where He fasted for forty days and nights and was tempted by the devil.

Jesus resisted every temptation by relying on God's word. He did not give in, even when He was hungry and physically weak.

After this time, Jesus began His public ministry. He travelled throughout the region teaching, healing the sick, welcoming people, and announcing the good news of God's kingdom.

The baptism at the Jordan marked the beginning of this important chapter in His earthly life.

What Can We Learn?

  • We should be willing to prepare ourselves for the work God gives us.
  • Humility means recognising that we are not always the most important person in a situation.
  • John the Baptist showed courage by sharing an important message with others.
  • Jesus demonstrated obedience and commitment to His mission.
  • Our actions can show what we believe, not just our words.
  • New beginnings can be important moments in our lives.

Think About It!

John knew that his role was to prepare the way for Jesus rather than seek attention for himself. Why can it sometimes be difficult to put others before ourselves? How can we practise humility in our everyday lives?
