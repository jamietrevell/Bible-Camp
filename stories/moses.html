COURAGE AND DELIVERANCE

Moses and the Exodus

Exodus 1–15

Born into a time of danger, raised in Pharaoh's palace, and called by God from a burning bush, Moses would become the man who led an entire nation out of slavery.

A Baby in a Basket

Many years after Joseph's time in Egypt, the Israelites had become a large population. A new Pharaoh came to power who did not remember Joseph or what he had done for Egypt.

Pharaoh feared that the Israelites were becoming too numerous. He forced them into harsh slavery and ordered that every newborn Hebrew boy should be thrown into the Nile River.

Around this time, a Hebrew woman gave birth to a son. She hid him for three months, but eventually she could no longer keep him concealed.

She placed him in a carefully prepared basket and set it among the reeds along the riverbank. His older sister, Miriam, watched from a distance.

Pharaoh's daughter discovered the baby. She felt compassion for him and decided to raise him as her own. Miriam approached her and offered to find a Hebrew woman to nurse the child.

The baby's own mother was brought to care for him. When he grew older, he was taken to Pharaoh's daughter, who named him Moses.

Moses Flees Egypt

Moses grew up in Pharaoh's household. One day, he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew slave. Moses intervened and killed the Egyptian.

When Pharaoh learned what had happened, he wanted Moses dead. Moses fled Egypt and travelled to the land of Midian.

There he helped the daughters of a priest named Jethro, married one of them, Zipporah, and began a new life as a shepherd.

The Burning Bush

One day, while tending his flock near Mount Horeb, Moses saw something extraordinary: a bush was on fire, but it was not being consumed by the flames.

As Moses approached, God called to him from the bush. God told him to remove his sandals because the ground where he stood was holy.

God explained that He had seen the suffering of the Israelites in Egypt and had heard their cries. He was sending Moses to Pharaoh to bring His people out of slavery.

Moses was afraid and questioned whether he was the right person for such an enormous responsibility. He worried that people would not listen to him.

God reassured him and promised to be with him. He also appointed Moses' brother Aaron to help him speak to Pharaoh.

Before Pharaoh

Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and delivered God's command: the Israelites were to be allowed to leave Egypt.

Pharaoh refused. He did not want to lose the people who worked for him, and he made their labour even harder.

The Israelites became discouraged, but God repeated His promise to deliver them.

The Ten Plagues

God sent a series of plagues upon Egypt to demonstrate His power and persuade Pharaoh to release the Israelites. Each time, Pharaoh resisted or changed his mind.

The plagues were:

  1. Water in the Nile and other water sources turned to blood.
  2. Frogs covered the land.
  3. Gnats or lice spread throughout Egypt.
  4. Swarms of flies filled the houses and land.
  5. A deadly disease struck Egyptian livestock.
  6. Painful boils appeared on people and animals.
  7. Hail destroyed crops and damaged the land.
  8. Locusts consumed what remained of the crops.
  9. Darkness covered Egypt for three days.
  10. The firstborn in Egyptian households died.

Before the final plague, God gave the Israelites instructions for a special meal. Each household was to sacrifice a lamb and mark the doorposts with its blood.

When the final plague came, the marked homes were passed over. This event became known as Passover and was to be remembered by the Israelites.

Freedom at Last

After the final plague, Pharaoh finally told Moses and Aaron to take the Israelites and leave Egypt. The people gathered their belongings and began their journey out of slavery.

But Pharaoh soon changed his mind. He gathered his army and chased after them.

The Israelites found themselves trapped between Pharaoh's approaching army and the Red Sea. They were frightened and cried out to Moses.

Crossing the Red Sea

God told Moses to stretch out his staff over the sea. A strong wind blew throughout the night, and the waters divided, leaving a path of dry ground.

The Israelites crossed through the sea, with walls of water on either side of them.

Pharaoh's army pursued them into the sea. But when the Israelites had reached the other side, Moses stretched out his hand again, and the waters returned to their place.

The Israelites were safe. They had escaped the army that had held them captive.

A Song of Freedom

The people rejoiced and praised God for their deliverance. Moses and the Israelites sang a song celebrating what God had done.

Miriam, Moses' sister, took a tambourine, and the women followed her with dancing and celebration.

Their journey was far from over, but they were no longer slaves in Egypt. They were free.

What Can We Learn?

  • Bravery does not mean never feeling afraid.
  • We can ask for help when a responsibility feels too big.
  • Standing up for people who are suffering matters.
  • Freedom and justice are worth protecting.
  • We can find hope even in frightening circumstances.

Think About It!

Moses did not believe he was capable of leading the Israelites at first. Have you ever doubted your ability to do something important? What helped you find the courage to try?
