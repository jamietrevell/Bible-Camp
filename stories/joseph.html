DREAMS AND FORGIVENESS

Joseph and His Brothers

Genesis 37–50

Joseph was his father's beloved son, a young man with extraordinary dreams. But jealousy, betrayal, and unexpected hardship would take him far from home before his story reached an incredible conclusion.

A Favourite Son

Joseph was one of the twelve sons of Jacob. He was especially loved by his father, who gave him a beautiful special robe. This made Joseph's brothers jealous, and they grew to resent him.

Their jealousy became worse when Joseph began telling them about his dreams. In one dream, he and his brothers were binding sheaves of grain in a field. Joseph's sheaf stood upright while his brothers' sheaves bowed down to it.

In another dream, the sun, moon, and eleven stars bowed down to him. His family understood that the dreams seemed to suggest that Joseph would one day rule over them.

His brothers hated hearing these dreams. Even his father questioned what they meant, although he remembered them.

Betrayed by His Brothers

One day, Jacob sent Joseph to check on his brothers, who were tending their father's flocks.

When they saw him approaching, they plotted against him. They called him the dreamer and discussed killing him.

Reuben, the eldest, tried to prevent them from murdering Joseph. Instead, the brothers stripped Joseph of his special robe and threw him into an empty pit.

Later, a group of traders travelling to Egypt came along. Joseph's brothers sold him to them for twenty pieces of silver.

They dipped Joseph's robe in goat's blood and took it to their father. Jacob recognised the robe and believed a wild animal had killed his beloved son. He mourned deeply.

Joseph in Egypt

Joseph was taken to Egypt and sold to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh. Although Joseph was far from home, he worked diligently, and Potiphar came to trust him with the management of his household.

But Joseph faced another injustice. Potiphar's wife falsely accused him after he refused her advances. Potiphar had Joseph thrown into prison.

Even in prison, Joseph did not give up. The prison official placed him in charge of the other prisoners. God was with Joseph through his difficult circumstances.

The Dreams in Prison

While Joseph was imprisoned, Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker were also brought there. Both men had troubling dreams.

Joseph explained their dreams, telling the cupbearer that he would be restored to his position and the baker that he would be executed.

Both interpretations came true. Joseph asked the cupbearer to remember him when he returned to Pharaoh's service, but the cupbearer forgot him.

Pharaoh's Dreams

Two years later, Pharaoh had two disturbing dreams. In the first, seven healthy cows were eaten by seven thin, unhealthy cows. In the second, seven healthy ears of grain were swallowed by seven thin ears.

None of Pharaoh's advisers could explain the dreams. Then the cupbearer remembered Joseph.

Joseph was brought before Pharaoh. He explained that both dreams meant the same thing: Egypt would experience seven years of abundant harvests, followed by seven years of severe famine.

Joseph advised Pharaoh to appoint someone to collect and store food during the plentiful years so that the country would survive the famine.

Pharaoh recognised Joseph's wisdom and appointed him as the second-highest authority in Egypt.

The Brothers Return

Just as Joseph had predicted, the famine came. People from surrounding lands travelled to Egypt to buy grain because Joseph had organised its storage.

Eventually, Joseph's own brothers arrived to buy food. They bowed before him, not recognising the powerful Egyptian official standing before them.

Joseph recognised them immediately. He remembered his dreams, but he did not reveal his identity at once. He questioned them and eventually asked them to bring their youngest brother, Benjamin, to Egypt.

After several encounters, Joseph could no longer hide his emotions. He sent the Egyptians out of the room and began to weep.

"I Am Joseph!"

Joseph finally revealed himself to his brothers. They were terrified, knowing what they had done to him years earlier.

But Joseph reassured them. He explained that although they had intended to harm him, God had used the events to preserve many lives during the famine.

Rather than seeking revenge, Joseph forgave his brothers. He invited them to bring their father and their families to Egypt, where they would have food and shelter.

A Family Reunited

Jacob was overjoyed when he learned that Joseph was alive. He travelled to Egypt with his family and was reunited with the son he had believed dead.

Joseph provided for his family throughout the famine. What had begun with jealousy and betrayal ended with forgiveness and reunion.

Joseph's life had taken him through many hardships, but he remained faithful. His story showed how unexpected circumstances could become part of something much bigger than he could see at the time.

What Can We Learn?

  • Jealousy can damage families and friendships.
  • Doing the right thing is important, even when it is difficult.
  • Hardship does not mean that hope is lost.
  • Forgiveness can help repair broken relationships.
  • We should not assume that difficult circumstances are the end of our story.

Think About It!

Joseph had the opportunity to take revenge on the brothers who betrayed him, but he chose forgiveness. Why do you think forgiveness can sometimes be harder than getting even?
