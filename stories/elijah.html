FAITH AND COURAGE

Elijah and the Fire from Heaven

1 Kings 17–19; 21; 2 Kings 2

At a time when many people in Israel had turned away from God, one prophet stood against a powerful king and an entire crowd of false prophets. His name was Elijah.

A Prophet in a Troubled Kingdom

After Solomon's reign, Israel became a divided kingdom. In the northern kingdom, a king named Ahab ruled alongside his wife, Jezebel.

Ahab did evil in the sight of God. He worshipped Baal, a false god, and encouraged the people of Israel to do the same.

Elijah the Tishbite was a prophet who delivered God's messages to the people. One day, he announced to Ahab that there would be no rain or dew in the land except at his word.

A severe drought followed, bringing hardship and famine. During this time, God provided for Elijah in unexpected ways.

Fed by Ravens

God instructed Elijah to hide beside the brook Cherith, east of the Jordan River.

There, ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning and evening, and he drank water from the brook.

Eventually, the brook dried up because there was no rain. God then instructed Elijah to travel to Zarephath, where a widow would provide for him.

The Widow of Zarephath

When Elijah arrived at Zarephath, he found a widow gathering sticks. He asked her for some water and a piece of bread.

The woman explained that she had only a little flour and oil left. She was preparing one final meal for herself and her son because she believed they would soon run out of food.

Elijah told her not to be afraid. He asked her to make him a small loaf first and promised that her flour and oil would not run out until God sent rain.

She trusted his words. Just as Elijah had said, the jar of flour and the jug of oil continued to provide for them.

Later, the widow's son became seriously ill and stopped breathing. Elijah prayed to God, and the boy's life returned to him. The widow recognised that Elijah was truly a man of God.

The Challenge on Mount Carmel

After a long period of drought, God sent Elijah back to Ahab. Elijah called the people of Israel and the prophets of Baal to gather on Mount Carmel.

He challenged the people to stop wavering between two opinions. If the Lord was God, they should follow Him. If Baal was god, they should follow Baal.

Elijah proposed a test. Two bulls would be prepared as sacrifices, one for Baal's prophets and one for Elijah. Neither side would set fire to its offering. Each would call upon its god, and the god who answered with fire would prove Himself.

The Prophets of Baal

The prophets of Baal prepared their sacrifice and called upon Baal from morning until noon. They shouted, danced around the altar, and continued their desperate cries.

But there was no answer. No fire came, and nothing happened.

Elijah then called the people closer. He repaired an old altar of the Lord using twelve stones, representing the twelve tribes of Israel.

He arranged the wood, placed the sacrifice on it, and instructed the people to pour water over everything. They did this repeatedly until water filled the trench around the altar.

Fire from Heaven

Elijah prayed to God, asking Him to answer so that the people would know that the Lord was God and that He was turning their hearts back to Him.

Suddenly, fire from the Lord fell. It consumed the sacrifice, the wood, the stones, and even the water in the trench.

The people fell facedown and cried, "The Lord, He is God! The Lord, He is God!"

Afterward, Elijah told Ahab that rain was coming. Soon the sky grew dark with clouds, and heavy rain fell across the land. The drought was over.

Elijah's Fear and Exhaustion

When Jezebel heard what had happened to the prophets of Baal, she threatened to kill Elijah. Frightened, Elijah fled into the wilderness.

He became exhausted and discouraged. He sat beneath a broom tree and asked God to let him die.

But God did not abandon him. An angel woke Elijah and provided bread and water. Elijah ate, drank, and rested. The food gave him strength for the journey ahead.

A Gentle Whisper

Elijah travelled to Mount Horeb, where he sheltered in a cave. God asked him why he was there.

Elijah explained that he felt alone and that the people of Israel had abandoned God's covenant.

God told him to stand on the mountain. A powerful wind came, followed by an earthquake and a fire. But God was not revealed to Elijah through those events.

After the fire came a gentle whisper. Elijah covered his face with his cloak and stood at the entrance of the cave.

God gave him further instructions and reminded him that he was not alone. There were still thousands in Israel who had not bowed to Baal.

Elijah's Final Journey

Elijah continued his prophetic work and eventually called Elisha to become his successor.

When the time came for Elijah to leave this world, he and Elisha travelled together. A chariot of fire and horses of fire appeared, separating them, and Elijah was taken up into heaven in a whirlwind.

Elisha watched in amazement and took up Elijah's fallen cloak. He would continue the prophetic work that Elijah had begun.

What Can We Learn?

  • Standing up for what is right can require courage.
  • God can provide for people in unexpected ways.
  • Feeling afraid or exhausted does not mean someone has failed.
  • Rest and care are important when we are struggling.
  • We are not always as alone as we might feel.
  • God's presence is not always found in dramatic events; sometimes it comes in quiet moments.

Think About It!

Elijah experienced an incredible victory on Mount Carmel, but later became frightened and exhausted. Why is it important to remember that even brave people sometimes need rest, comfort, and help?
