FAITH AND COURAGE

Daniel and the Lions' Den

Daniel 1–6

Far from his homeland, a young man named Daniel remained faithful to God. Even when powerful rulers tried to change his ways, Daniel refused to abandon what he believed.

Taken to Babylon

Many years after the reign of King Solomon, the kingdom of Judah was conquered by Babylon. King Nebuchadnezzar ordered that some of the young people from Judah be brought to his palace to be trained for service in his kingdom.

Among them were Daniel and three of his friends: Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. They were given new Babylonian names: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

The young men were offered food and wine from the king's table. However, Daniel was determined not to defile himself with the royal food. He respectfully asked the official in charge to give them vegetables and water instead.

The official was worried that they would become weak, but Daniel suggested a ten-day test. At the end of the ten days, Daniel and his friends looked healthier than the others. They were allowed to continue their chosen diet.

God gave the four young men knowledge and wisdom. Daniel also received the ability to understand visions and dreams.

The King's Mysterious Dream

One night, King Nebuchadnezzar had a troubling dream. He called his wise men and demanded that they tell him both the dream and its meaning. None of them could do it.

The king became furious and ordered that the wise men of Babylon be executed. Daniel heard about the order and asked for time to discover the meaning of the dream.

Daniel and his friends prayed together, asking God for mercy and wisdom. During the night, God revealed the mystery to Daniel in a vision.

Daniel went before the king and explained that no human being could reveal such a mystery, but God in heaven could.

He described the king's dream of a great statue with a head of gold, chest and arms of silver, belly and thighs of bronze, legs of iron, and feet made partly of iron and partly of clay.

Daniel explained that the statue represented a series of kingdoms. A stone, not cut by human hands, struck the statue and became a great mountain, representing a kingdom established by God that would never be destroyed.

The king was amazed. He honoured Daniel and appointed him to a high position in Babylon. Daniel also requested important positions for Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

The Fiery Furnace

Later, King Nebuchadnezzar made a huge golden statue and ordered everyone to bow down and worship it whenever music played.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused. They would worship only God.

The king was furious and ordered that they be brought before him. He gave them another chance to bow down, warning them that they would be thrown into a blazing furnace if they refused.

The three friends remained firm. They told the king that their God was able to rescue them from the furnace, but even if He did not, they would still refuse to worship the statue.

The king ordered the furnace to be heated seven times hotter than usual. The three young men were tied up and thrown inside. The flames were so intense that the soldiers who carried them towards the furnace died.

But when the king looked into the furnace, he was astonished. He saw four figures walking freely in the flames instead of three.

He called the young men out. They emerged unharmed. Their clothes were not burned, their hair was not singed, and they did not even smell of smoke.

The king praised their God and recognised that no other god could rescue in such a way.

Daniel's Life Under New Kings

Years passed, and Babylon eventually fell to the Persians. Daniel continued serving under the new rulers because of his wisdom, honesty, and excellent character.

Under King Darius, Daniel became one of the highest officials in the kingdom. The king even considered placing him over the entire empire.

The other officials became jealous. They searched for a reason to accuse Daniel, but they could find no dishonesty or corruption in his work.

They realised that the only way to trap him would be through his faith in God.

A Law Against Prayer

The officials persuaded King Darius to sign a law stating that for thirty days nobody could pray to any god or person except the king. Anyone who disobeyed would be thrown into a den of lions.

Daniel heard about the law, but he did not stop praying. Three times each day, he went to his upstairs room, opened his windows towards Jerusalem, and prayed to God, just as he had always done.

The officials found him praying and reported him to the king.

King Darius was deeply upset. He respected Daniel and tried desperately to find a way to save him. But the law could not be changed.

Into the Lions' Den

Daniel was brought to the den of lions. Before he was thrown inside, the king said that he hoped Daniel's God would rescue him.

A stone was placed over the entrance, and the king sealed it with his own signet ring. That night, Darius could not sleep. He refused entertainment and spent the night fasting.

At dawn, the king hurried to the lions' den. He called out anxiously, asking Daniel whether his God had been able to save him.

Daniel answered that God had sent an angel to shut the lions' mouths. They had not harmed him because he was innocent before God and had done no wrong against the king.

The king was overjoyed. Daniel was lifted out of the den, and everyone saw that he had not been injured.

A King Gives Praise

King Darius ordered that the men who had accused Daniel be punished for their plot. He then sent a message throughout his kingdom, declaring that everyone should honour Daniel's living God.

The king praised God as the One who rescues and saves, who performs signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, and who had rescued Daniel from the power of the lions.

Daniel continued to prosper during the reign of Darius and into the reign of Cyrus the Persian. Throughout his life, he had remained faithful to God, even when doing so was dangerous.

What Can We Learn?

  • We can remain faithful to our beliefs even when others disagree.
  • Honesty and integrity matter, even when nobody is watching.
  • Prayer can help us find strength during difficult times.
  • True courage means standing firm when doing so is frightening.
  • We should not let jealousy or pride lead us to hurt others.
  • God's people can trust Him even when they face uncertainty.

Think About It!

Daniel continued praying even when he knew it could get him into serious trouble. What does his story teach us about staying true to our values when other people pressure us to change?
