SACRIFICE AND LOVE

The Crucifixion of Jesus

Matthew 26–27; Mark 14–15; Luke 22–23; John 18–19

Jesus had taught people about God's love, healed the sick, and welcomed those who were rejected. But now, His enemies were determined to put Him to death.

Jesus Is Arrested

After sharing the Passover meal with His disciples, Jesus went with them to a garden called Gethsemane to pray.

He knew that difficult things were about to happen. He prayed to God, asking that the suffering ahead might be taken away, but ultimately submitted Himself to God's will.

While Jesus was praying, Judas Iscariot, one of His twelve disciples, arrived with a crowd carrying swords and clubs. Judas had agreed to identify Jesus to them with a kiss.

Jesus was arrested and taken away. His disciples scattered, frightened and confused.

Jesus Faces His Accusers

Jesus was brought before the religious authorities, who questioned Him and accused Him of wrongdoing.

Meanwhile, Peter, one of His closest disciples, followed at a distance. When people recognised him as one of Jesus' followers, Peter became afraid and denied knowing Him three times.

When he realised what he had done, Peter went away and wept bitterly.

Jesus was eventually taken before Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor. Although Pilate did not find a proper reason to condemn Him, the crowd demanded that Jesus be crucified.

The Road to the Cross

The soldiers mocked Jesus, dressed Him in a purple robe, and placed a crown made of thorns on His head. They laughed at Him and called Him the King of the Jews.

They struck Him and treated Him cruelly. Then they led Him away to be crucified.

Jesus was made to carry His cross towards a place called Golgotha, which means the Place of the Skull. Along the way, a man named Simon of Cyrene was made to help carry it.

Jesus Is Crucified

At Golgotha, the soldiers crucified Jesus between two criminals. They placed a sign above Him that read, "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews."

Some people mocked Him, telling Him to save Himself if He really was the Son of God. Others watched in sadness.

Even while suffering, Jesus showed compassion. According to Luke's Gospel, He prayed for those who were crucifying Him, asking God to forgive them.

One of the criminals beside Him recognised that Jesus had done nothing wrong. He asked Jesus to remember him when He came into His kingdom.

Jesus promised him that he would be with Him in paradise.

Jesus' Final Moments

As the hours passed, darkness came over the land. Jesus cried out to God and eventually said, "It is finished." Then He bowed His head and died.

At that moment, the curtain of the Temple was torn in two, from top to bottom. The earth shook, and rocks split.

A Roman centurion who witnessed what happened was filled with awe and declared that Jesus truly was the Son of God.

Jesus Is Buried

A man named Joseph of Arimathea, who was a follower of Jesus, asked Pilate for permission to take His body.

He wrapped Jesus' body in a clean linen cloth and placed it in a new tomb carved out of rock.

A large stone was rolled across the entrance of the tomb. Mary Magdalene and other women who followed Jesus saw where He had been buried.

The disciples were heartbroken. They had believed Jesus was the promised Messiah, but now He had been killed.

They did not yet understand what would happen next.

Why the Crucifixion Matters

Christians believe that Jesus willingly gave His life as a sacrifice for humanity's sins. His death is central to the Christian understanding of God's love, mercy, and plan for salvation.

The crucifixion was not the end of the story. Three days later, according to the Gospels, something extraordinary happened at the tomb.

What Can We Learn?

  • Jesus showed love and compassion even while suffering.
  • Fear can sometimes lead people to make choices they later regret, as Peter discovered.
  • Forgiveness is an important part of Jesus' teaching and example.
  • Christians understand Jesus' sacrifice as the greatest expression of God's love for humanity.
  • Even in moments of sadness, the Christian story points towards hope.
