FAITH AND OBEDIENCE

Noah and the Great Flood

Genesis 6–9

In a world filled with wickedness, one man chose to follow God. His name was Noah, and God gave him an extraordinary task that would change the course of human history.

A World That Had Forgotten God

As the human population grew, wickedness spread across the earth. People behaved cruelly, and violence filled the world. God saw how terrible things had become, and He was grieved by the evil people were doing.

But one man stood out. Noah was a righteous man who walked faithfully with God. He was different from the people around him.

God decided to send a great flood to cleanse the earth of its wickedness. But He would preserve Noah, his family, and the animals through an enormous vessel called an ark.

An Extraordinary Assignment

God told Noah to build an ark from gopher wood. He gave him specific instructions about its length, width, height, and construction. The ark would have different levels and a door in its side.

Noah was also instructed to bring his family aboard and take animals with him so that life could continue after the flood. He was to bring pairs of animals, male and female, and provisions for everyone.

It was a huge undertaking. Building such a vessel required patience, effort, and trust. Noah followed God's instructions and did everything God commanded.

Entering the Ark

When the time came, Noah entered the ark with his wife, his three sons — Shem, Ham, and Japheth — and their wives.

The animals came aboard too. Birds, livestock, and other creatures entered according to God's instructions. Food was stored for the long journey.

Then God shut the door.

The Great Flood

Rain began to fall. It did not stop after a few hours or even a few days. Rain poured down for forty days and forty nights.

The waters rose higher and higher until even the mountains were covered. Everything outside the ark that depended on dry land perished.

Inside the ark, Noah and his family waited. The vessel floated upon the waters, carrying the people and animals who had been preserved.

The floodwaters covered the earth for a long time. Eventually, God remembered Noah and caused a wind to pass over the earth. The waters began to recede.

The Raven and the Dove

After many days, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat. Noah waited until the tops of the mountains became visible.

He sent out a raven, which flew back and forth. Then he sent out a dove to see whether the waters had gone down.

At first, the dove found nowhere to land and returned to Noah. He waited seven more days before sending it out again.

This time, the dove returned with a freshly plucked olive leaf in its beak. Noah understood that the waters were receding and that plants were beginning to appear again.

After another seven days, Noah sent the dove out once more. This time, it did not return.

A New Beginning

Eventually, the earth became dry. God told Noah that he, his family, and all the animals could leave the ark.

Noah built an altar to God and offered sacrifices. God was pleased with Noah's worship.

God blessed Noah and his sons, telling them to be fruitful, multiply, and fill the earth.

The Rainbow Promise

God made a covenant with Noah, his descendants, and every living creature. He promised that He would never again destroy all life on earth with a flood.

And He gave them a sign of that promise: a rainbow in the clouds.

Whenever the rainbow appeared, it would remind generations to come of God's covenant with the earth.

What Can We Learn?

  • We should remain faithful even when others around us make wrong choices.
  • Obedience sometimes requires patience and perseverance.
  • God's promises are important and should be remembered.
  • Every new beginning is an opportunity to live differently.
  • We should care for the living creatures and world around us.

Think About It!

Noah had to trust God with an enormous task, even before he could see how everything would turn out. Can you think of a time when doing the right thing required patience or courage?
