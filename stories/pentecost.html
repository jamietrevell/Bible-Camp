THE EARLY CHURCH

The Coming of the Holy Spirit

Acts 2:1–47

Jesus had promised His disciples that they would receive the Holy Spirit. They waited together in Jerusalem, not knowing exactly what would happen next. Then, on the day of Pentecost, an extraordinary event changed their lives forever.

Waiting in Jerusalem

After Jesus ascended into heaven, His disciples returned to Jerusalem as He had instructed. They gathered together, praying and waiting for the promise of the Holy Spirit.

It was the day of Pentecost, a Jewish festival celebrated fifty days after Passover. Jerusalem was filled with visitors from many different places.

A Sound Like a Mighty Wind

Suddenly, a sound like a powerful rushing wind came from heaven and filled the house where the disciples were sitting.

They saw what seemed to be tongues of fire separating and resting on each of them.

All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began speaking in other languages, as the Spirit enabled them.

People Hear Their Own Languages

The visitors in Jerusalem heard the noise and gathered to discover what was happening. They were amazed because each person heard the disciples speaking in their own language.

The crowd included people from many regions and nations. They could hardly believe that these disciples were speaking languages they had grown up hearing.

Some people were amazed and wanted to know what it all meant. Others mocked the disciples and suggested that they had been drinking.

Peter Speaks to the Crowd

Peter stood up with the other apostles and addressed the crowd.

He explained that the disciples were not drunk. Instead, what the people were witnessing was the fulfilment of God's promise, spoken through the prophet Joel.

Peter spoke about Jesus, explaining His life, death, and resurrection. He told the crowd that God had raised Jesus from the dead and that the disciples were witnesses to this truth.

His words deeply affected many of the listeners. They asked Peter and the other apostles what they should do.

Thousands Believe

Peter told them to repent and be baptised in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of their sins. He also spoke of the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Many people accepted his message. About three thousand people were baptised that day and joined the growing community of believers.

The First Christian Community

The new believers devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, fellowship, sharing meals, and prayer.

They helped one another and shared what they had with people in need. They met together regularly, worshipped God, and enjoyed spending time as a community.

Their faith was not simply something they talked about. It shaped the way they treated one another.

More people continued to join them, and the Christian community grew.

Why Pentecost Matters

Christians celebrate Pentecost because they believe it marks the coming of the Holy Spirit upon the disciples and the beginning of the Church's public mission.

The disciples who had once been frightened and uncertain now spoke boldly about Jesus. They began sharing the message with people from different nations and backgrounds.

Pentecost reminds Christians that God's message is for people everywhere and that the Holy Spirit helps believers live out their faith.

What Can We Learn?

  • God's promises give us hope and courage.
  • People from different languages and backgrounds can come together in faith.
  • The Holy Spirit gives Christians strength to live out their beliefs.
  • Faith should be reflected in kindness, generosity, and care for others.
  • We should not be afraid to share messages of hope and love.

Think About It!

Why do you think it was important that people from so many different nations heard the disciples speaking in their own languages? How can we make others feel welcome, regardless of where they come from?
